India note. AREAER 2023 Table 4 places India under "Floating". The IMF Overview states that floating arrangements fell by 3 to 32 and stabilized arrangements fell by 1 to 22 (search-engine excerpt of the primary PDF). The Bundesbank list counts exactly 32 floating (India included) and 22 stabilized (India excluded), so the counts reconcile only with India in the floating row. Two IMF statements conflict with that table entry. IMF Country Report 23/426 (December 2023, primary: https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2023/english/1indea2023001.pdf.md) says staff reclassified India's de facto regime from floating to "stabilized arrangement" for December 2022-October 2023, with the de jure classification remaining floating. A secondary source (The Leap Blog, May 2026: https://blog.theleapjournal.org/2026/05/words-and-deeds-in-indian-exchange-rate.html) quotes the 2023 AREAER as saying the arrangement was "reclassified retroactively to 'stabilized' from 'floating', effective December 6, 2022." That wording most likely comes from the AREAER 2023 India country chapter (AREAER Online), which I could not open. The table and the retroactive reclassification can coexist because the IMF method needs six months of data for a stabilized classification: on 30 April 2023 the post-December-2022 stability was under five months old, and the AREAER 2023 Overview itself (search excerpt) explains that changes too recent for six months of data are left to the next edition. The retroactive reclassification should therefore appear in the change table of the next AREAER (May 2023-April 2024); I found no evidence that edition is published.

## (c) Differences from areaer_non_floating_spells.csv

1. Russia, start. The IMF dates the conventional peg to a composite from February 1, 2006 (CR 08/309, retroactive). The CSV starts in 2007 and its note says the switch date between 8/06 and 4/08 is unpublished; CR 08/309 publishes it. The CSV note's "conventional peg ... then stabilized then other managed" matches the IMF sequence, with the stabilized piece dated April 30 to November 1, 2008. The end (floating effective November 10, 2014) matches the CSV's 2014.
2. Switzerland, second spell. The IMF dates the crawl-like arrangement from May 19, 2020 (CR 21/130). The CSV records only 2021. The end, January 7, 2022, matches the CSV note "floating from 1/22". If the rule used the as-of-April-30 tables, the April 30, 2022 table most likely still showed crawl-like (search excerpt only).
3. Switzerland, first spell. The CSV label "other managed then crawl-like" omits the stabilized sub-spell (January 1, 2012 to January 14, 2013, retroactive). The crawl-like arrangement ran to January 15, 2015, two weeks past the CSV's last year of 2014.
4. Indonesia, missing spell. AREAER 2012 records a stabilized arrangement that ended February 14, 2011; the CSV has no 2010-2011 Indonesia row. Its start date is not in any source I could open; the 2010 Article IV (data to mid-2010) still said floating, so the spell most likely began in the second half of 2010 or was set retroactively by AREAER 2011.
5. India, start and content. The IMF dates the stabilized arrangement from December 2022 (December 6, 2022 per the secondary quote of the AREAER). The CSV starts in 2023. The IMF stabilized window runs through November 2024 (CR 25/54); the crawl-like start date is unverified. Separately, AREAER 2023 Table 4 still lists India as floating as of 30 April 2023, so the rule 1 table and the retroactive spell disagree about the same date.
6. Euro area. The 2003-2006 IMF tables classify euro area members as "no separate legal tender". The CSV has no euro area row. This is a definitional point: the paper's rule presumably classifies the euro, which the IMF describes as floating freely.
7. Hungary and Israel. The CSV years are consistent with every IMF table read (band through July 2006 and floating by April 2008 for Hungary; crawling band in 2003 and floating by June 2004 for Israel). The IMF gives no effective dates for either, and no IMF table between July 2006 and April 2008 exists in the public series, so Hungary's 2007 and both countries' 2001-2002 rest on the CSV, not on a table I read.
8. Czechia (November 7, 2013 to April 6, 2017) and Indonesia's 2012-2013 and 2017 spells match the CSV.
